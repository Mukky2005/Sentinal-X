Tenant-Isolated Enterprise Knowledge Assistant

MVP Scope  |  Version 1.0  |  9 November 2026

Working product definition for the first demonstrable release

PRODUCT PURPOSEAllow each company to manage its own internal knowledge documents and give employees role-appropriate answers without exposing documents assigned to other roles or tenants.

Document status: MVP product and engineering baseline. Items marked as future work are not required for the first release.

1. Executive Summary

The Tenant-Isolated Enterprise Knowledge Assistant is a web application through which a company can provide employees with question-answering access to internal policies, procedures, and reference documents. Each participating company is represented as a tenant. A company administrator manages that tenant, uploads documents once, assigns each document to the employee role or roles allowed to read it, and creates or manages employee accounts.

For the MVP, the example tenant is Company ABC. Company ABC has one Tenant Administrator and employees assigned to roles such as Tech Employee, Finance Employee, and Business Analyst. When an employee asks a question, the backend identifies the employee and their tenant and role, retrieves only the document chunks permitted for that role, and then sends the authorized context to a locally hosted model through Ollama. The answer is generated from that allowed context.

The central product rule is: tenant and role authorization must be applied before retrieved content is passed to the language model. A system prompt may remind the model to respect the user role, but it is not the security boundary. The database query and backend authorization logic enforce which chunks can be retrieved.

2. Product Description

2.1 Product vision

Give employees a simple, role-aware way to ask questions about their company’s internal knowledge, while allowing the company administrator to manage documents and decide which employee roles may access them.

2.2 What the product provides

A public main page that explains the product, what it provides, and how companies use it.

A tenant workspace for each participating company, with Company ABC used as the demonstrator tenant.

A company-admin dashboard for creating roles, managing employees, uploading documents, and assigning document access.

Role-based employee access for Tech Employees, Finance Employees, and Business Analysts.

Document extraction, application-side chunking, local embedding generation, and vector storage.

A chat interface that answers questions using only chunks authorized for the authenticated employee.

Tenant- and role-aware conversation handling and basic audit events.

2.3 Problem being addressed

Company policies and internal procedures may apply only to particular employee groups. A technical employee may need engineering standards and IT procedures, while a finance employee may need reimbursement and payment policies. A basic RAG chatbot that searches all documents in a shared collection can return information that is relevant to the question but not permitted for the employee. The product prevents this by attaching tenant and role-access metadata to indexed chunks and applying those restrictions during retrieval.

2.4 Product boundary

The MVP demonstrates one tenant workspace with a design that supports multiple tenants. Company ABC is the example company, not a hard-coded special case. Each tenant has its own tenant identifier, users, role definitions, documents, chunks, and conversations. The first demonstration may use a small number of tenant environments, but the data and authorization model must not assume that all users belong to Company ABC.

3. Tenant Model and Identity

3.1 Tenant definition

A tenant is one customer company using the platform. For example, Company ABC is one tenant. Its Tenant Administrator and all employees are members of the same tenant, but their role assignments determine which role-scoped documents they can retrieve.

3.2 Tenant identifier

When a company tenant is created, the backend generates a unique, random, non-guessable tenant identifier (preferably a UUID). This tenant_id is stored with tenant-owned records and is attached to each document and chunk during ingestion. It is also used in server-side tenant resolution and retrieval filtering.

The tenant_id is an identifier, not a password or authorization token. Employees should not gain access merely by knowing or typing the tenant_id. The administrator invites or registers an employee through a tenant invitation or enrollment process; after authentication, the backend binds that user account to the tenant. If a join code is used, it must be a separate, revocable invitation credential. The actual tenant_id is resolved by the server from the authenticated user’s membership.

3.3 Tenant boundary

A user account belongs to a tenant through a server-side membership record.

A tenant’s admin can manage only that tenant’s employees, roles, and documents.

A tenant’s employee can retrieve only chunks belonging to that tenant and allowed for the employee’s assigned role.

The tenant_id is stored on document and chunk metadata even when separate databases are used, providing an additional consistency check.

The backend must not trust tenant_id values supplied only in a browser request or natural-language prompt.

6. Document Upload and Role-Scoped Knowledge

6.1 Upload once; assign access by role

The administrator uploads a source document once. During upload, the administrator selects the employee role or roles allowed to access that document. The platform does not require a separate duplicate upload for every employee. For example, a technical policy document can be uploaded once and assigned to Tech Employee. A finance policy document can be uploaded once and assigned to Finance Employee. A company-wide policy can be assigned to “All tenant employees.”

A document can be assigned to one role, multiple roles, or all employee roles. The selected access scope is stored as metadata and inherited by the chunks produced from that document. In the initial MVP, access is assigned at document level; all chunks from a document inherit the same role scope. Fine-grained section-by-section permissions within a single mixed-audience document are outside the first MVP unless a concrete requirement is added.

6.2 Processing pipeline: extraction, chunking, embedding, storage

1.  The administrator uploads a supported document through the Company ABC tenant workspace.

2.  The FastAPI backend validates the user, tenant membership, upload permission, file extension/type, and configured file-size limit.

3.  The document-processing component extracts text from the file. Apache Tika may be used for PDF, DOCX, and other supported formats; supported formats must be verified in implementation tests.

4.  The backend applies deterministic text chunking, using configured chunk size and overlap. Chunking is performed by the application text-splitting pipeline; it is not inherently an LLM task.

5.  Each chunk is assigned metadata including tenant_id, document_id, document_version, allowed_role_ids (or equivalent mapping), source title, and page/section information where available.

6.  A local Ollama embedding model converts each chunk into a vector embedding.

7.  The chunk text, embedding, source metadata, tenant_id, and role-access mapping are stored in the tenant’s PostgreSQL database with pgvector enabled. PostgreSQL/pgvector can run as a Docker container for local development and the MVP deployment.

8.  The administrator sees an indexed or failed status. Failed ingestion must not leave partially indexed content available to employees.

6.3 Correct role for Ollama

Ollama is used in the MVP to run local models. An embedding model is used to generate vectors for document chunks and query text. A local chat/generation model can be used to produce the final answer from authorized retrieved context. Text chunking itself is normally handled by the backend splitter after extraction. This separation makes chunk size, overlap, and metadata handling deterministic and testable.

6.4 Docker and vector database

Docker is used to pull and run the PostgreSQL image that includes the pgvector extension, or a compatible PostgreSQL/pgvector image. Docker runs the database service; PostgreSQL stores the actual chunk records, metadata, and vectors in a persistent volume. The vector data must survive container restarts. Ollama may run locally on the development host or as a separately managed container, depending on the development environment.

7. Employee Query and Retrieval Workflow

7.1 Required query flow

1.  The employee signs in. The backend validates the authenticated identity.

2.  The backend resolves the employee’s tenant_id and active role from server-side membership records.

3.  The query is embedded using the same compatible embedding model used for document chunks.

4.  The backend selects the correct tenant database and performs vector similarity search with mandatory tenant and role filters.

5.  Only chunks matching tenant_id and the employee’s allowed role scope are returned.

6.  The backend constructs the LLM context from those authorized chunks only.

7.  A system instruction tells the local chat model to answer using the supplied evidence, not invent policy, and say when the authorized context is insufficient.

8.  Ollama generates the answer. The backend returns the answer and available source references.

9.  The question, answer, and source references are stored under the correct tenant and user, and only that user (or an explicitly authorized tenant admin) can access the conversation according to the MVP policy.

7.2 Mandatory retrieval rule

The effective retrieval condition is: the chunk must belong to the tenant resolved for the authenticated user AND the chunk must be assigned to the user’s role or to the “All tenant employees” scope. These conditions are applied in the backend/database query before the chunks are supplied to Ollama.

7.3 System prompt is not the access-control boundary

The model’s system instruction should include the user’s role context and direct the model to answer only from supplied authorized evidence. However, the role restriction must not depend on the model obeying that prompt. Unauthorized chunks must be removed by backend/database filtering before prompt construction. A system prompt cannot reliably protect a chunk that has already been supplied to the model.

9.3 Retrieval rule (logical form)

For an employee request, the retrieval query must enforce both tenant membership and role permission. Conceptually: tenant_id = authenticated_user.tenant_id AND (the chunk is assigned to authenticated_user.role_id OR the chunk has ALL_TENANT_EMPLOYEES visibility). Similarity ranking is applied only within this authorized candidate set. The exact SQL and schema may vary, but the access condition is mandatory.

10.1 Data isolation for MVP

The intended deployment model is a dedicated PostgreSQL database for each tenant. For the first Company ABC demonstration, one database can represent Company ABC. Tenant-specific configuration selects the correct database after server-side membership resolution. If a multi-tenant test environment is used, it must create separate tenant database instances or schemas only if that alternative is explicitly documented; role filters alone must not be presented as equivalent to dedicated database isolation.

10.2 Ollama responsibilities

Embedding model: generates vector embeddings for document chunks and query text. The same embedding model/version and vector dimensionality must be used consistently for indexing and query embedding.

Chat model: receives only the context returned by the authorized retrieval query and generates the natural-language response.

Chunking: performed by the backend text-splitting code after extraction. Ollama is not required to divide the document into chunks.

10.3 Docker responsibilities

Docker pulls and runs a PostgreSQL image with pgvector enabled. PostgreSQL is the vector database; Docker is the container runtime, not the database itself. Database files must be persisted in a Docker volume so data survives a container restart. Ollama may run as a local host service or a separately managed container.

13.1 MVP success definition

The MVP is ready for demonstration when a company administrator can create or manage the four initial role types, add employees, upload a document once, assign its permitted role(s), and observe successful ingestion. Employees must be able to ask questions and receive answers only from documents assigned to their role or shared with all tenant employees. The decisive demonstration is that a Tech Employee asking for a Finance-only policy does not cause Finance chunks to enter the retrieval result or the Ollama prompt.

14. Out of Scope for the MVP

NVIDIA TensorRT-LLM, NVIDIA Dynamo, and dynamic routing across multiple inference engines.

Agentic workflows that execute external actions, create tickets, approve payments, or modify enterprise systems.

Advanced multi-agent orchestration and long-term agent memory.

Neo4j or other knowledge graph retrieval, hybrid search, and advanced reranking.

Audio, video, image, and other multimodal ingestion beyond text extraction supported by the chosen parsers.

Large domain-specific canonical-intent libraries and complex NeMo Guardrails flows. Basic deterministic checks may be added, but guardrails are not the access-control layer.

Advanced persona management. The MVP may display role-specific navigation, but personas do not grant permissions.

Fine-grained different permissions for separate sections inside a single document. The MVP assigns access at document level and propagates that scope to all its chunks.

Formal certification, claims of zero leakage, or claims of complete prompt-injection prevention.

17. Product Principles

The Tenant Administrator manages one company tenant, not the entire platform.

A tenant ID is an identifier; authentication and server-side membership establish access.

A document is uploaded once and assigned to one or more permitted roles.

Every indexed chunk retains its tenant and role-access metadata.

Role and tenant filters are applied before the LLM receives context.

The model is instructed to answer from authorized evidence, but the model prompt is not the enforcement mechanism.

Ollama provides local embedding and chat model execution; the backend performs document chunking.

Docker runs the PostgreSQL/pgvector service; PostgreSQL persists the records and vectors.

If authorization cannot be verified, the request is rejected rather than searched broadly.
